THE OPERATING MANUAL

From drawing to a verified cut list.

A practical guide for designers, estimators, drawing reviewers and factory users. Use it as the shared reference for operating DCODE Low Balance—from 2D/3D design through engineering and cut-list review—and when to stop and verify.

Golden ruleImported dimensions are candidates until checked against the original drawing. A 2D/3D preview, collision check or completed checklist is not manufacturing certification.

The safest end-to-end workflow

  1. Choose your route. Design Studio for a new cabinet or room design; Panel Geometry for one measured panel; Assembly Builder for a multi-panel assembly; Cutting List Preparator when you already have a drawing.
  2. Name the job and keep backups. Use clear job, room and unit names. The standalone app saves work in this browser's local storage and does not automatically sync across devices.
  3. Enter measured dimensions. Design/geometry fields use millimetres unless stated otherwise. Verify site dimensions, openings, levels, wall conditions, fillers, appliance clearances and obstructions.
  4. Configure construction. Specify board thickness, back style, shelves, partitions, shutters, drawers, lofts, profiles and accessories. Use exact hardware models and current manufacturer drawings.
  5. Compare 2D and 3D. Inspect plan, main/return elevation, side/section and 3D for orientation, missing parts, collisions, gaps and dimension disagreements.
  6. Review every cut-list row. Check length, width, thickness, quantity, material/grade, decor code, finish, grain, edge band, liner/backer and machining notes. Resolve every unknown.
  7. Regenerate after geometry changes. Rebuild the list from the current design, then inspect it again. Investigate duplicate manual/imported rows.
  8. Export only after review. Keep CSV/SVG and project backup beside the source drawing and revision. Obtain authorised human approval before manufacture.

1. Design Studio — new cabinets and room furniture

Follow the guided wizard in order:

  1. Project — name the job.
  2. Space & cabinet — identify room/zone and unit.
  3. Room layout & unit templates — choose unit template and room arrangement where applicable.
  4. Dimensions & room fit — enter measured dimensions and review clearances.
  5. Accessories — choose fittings; unknown models remain unverified.
  6. Components — inspect shutters, shelves, drawers, partitions and custom components.
  7. Materials & hardware specs — specify board, grade, finish, decor code, edge band, hinge/runner/connector and drilling references.
  8. Design review & release — compare views and inspect every manufacturing row.

If the wizard blocks progression, correct the named field using a measured value. Do not guess just to continue.

2. Panel Geometry — one panel

Use Dimensions for a rectangular panel or 3-point geometry for measured 3D points. Set part name, plane/orientation, thickness and offsets. Inspect Front/Face, Plan/Top and Side/Edge; then switch to 3D orbit and adjust yaw/elevation to inspect orientation and extrusion. Export the 2D SVG if needed.

Add panel to Cutlist sends an unapproved proposal. Check material, grain, edge treatment and finished-versus-cut-size convention before approving.

3. Assembly Builder — multi-panel assembly

Choose a preset, enter overall dimensions and construction parameters, then build it. Review the constraint report, component list, front/plan/side views and 3D assembly. For manual objects, verify dimensions, orientation and lower-corner coordinates.

Board panels transfer to the board cut list; mirror, glass and hardware belong to separate schedules. A zero-intersection report does not prove joinery, screw positions, pilot holes, connector locations or hardware compliance.

4. Manufacturing Cutlist — part-by-part review

Treat this as a manufacturing review queue. Check dimensions and quantity first, then material and finish data. Unknowns must remain marked for verification. Fill all fields affecting manufacture on manual rows. Before export, confirm the list is current and includes only intended source rows.

5. Cutting List Preparator — existing drawings

Start from the real source file, not example data. Import options shown in the app include DXF, SVG, PDF, PNG/JPG and DWG. Prefer CAD vector files where available; convert DWG to DXF if necessary. Review multi-page drawings page by page, accept only relevant pages, then inspect the consolidated list.

Treat extracted labels and dimensions as candidates. Confirm them against the original drawing, scale, dimension line and part outline. OCR/AI suggestions are not authoritative measurements. Do not automatically delete repeated dimensions; investigate whether they belong to another view or part.

How to verify 2D against 3D

  • Confirm both views use the same unit and design revision.
  • Compare overall width, height and depth first.
  • Check board thickness, back style, shelves, partitions, lofts, doors, drawers and gaps.
  • Confirm orientation and which side dimensions are measured from.
  • After changing a geometry-driving input, wait for redraw and re-check the relevant views and cut list.
  • If drawing, 3D and cut-list dimensions disagree, stop and do not export. Save a backup and report the exact inputs with a screenshot.

A 3D preview is a coordination aid, not a finish sample or machining simulation.

Materials, finishes and hardware

  • Material/grade: specify the actual board and verify supplier datasheet.
  • Surface/decor: record brand, collection, exact decor code and finish. Screen colour is only a visual hint.
  • Grain: specify direction per part and check cutting/nesting orientation.
  • Edge band: specify exposed edges, thickness and matching colour/code.
  • Liner/backer: include only when the drawing/specification calls for it.
  • Hinges/sliding systems/runners: record exact model and current manufacturer clearances, overlays, drilling pattern and limits.
  • Machining: verify boring, grooves, connectors, pilot holes and profiles against an approved manufacturing drawing when not generated by the app.

Save, backup and restore

The standalone app uses browser-local storage. Work may be lost if site data is cleared and does not automatically follow you to another device.

  1. Keep original CAD/PDF files in a named job folder outside the browser.
  2. Export project/design JSON where available.
  3. Save CSV and SVG alongside the source drawing and revision.
  4. After restore/import, verify project identity, dimensions, part count, material fields and source revision.
  5. Do not assume separate workspaces share identical project state; confirm each panel/assembly handoff reached the intended cut list.

Pre-release checklist

  • Correct job, room, unit and drawing revision.
  • Site dimensions, openings and obstructions checked.
  • 2D views compared with the 3D preview.
  • Every part's dimensions, thickness and quantity checked.
  • Material, grade, decor, finish, grain, edge band and liner/backer confirmed or explicitly held for verification.
  • Exact hardware models, clearances and machining references checked.
  • Cut list regenerated after geometry changes; duplicates and retained imported/manual rows investigated.
  • CSV/SVG/project backup saved with the source drawing.
  • Responsible human approval obtained before factory/CNC release.
Never manufacture from an unreviewed extraction or preview.If a value is unknown, a view disagrees, or the drawing revision is uncertain, stop and verify.
